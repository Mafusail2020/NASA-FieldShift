# Winter wheat
Sown late September to mid October, harvested July. The crop needs cold winter hardening and is most sensitive to drought in spring (tillering to booting, April-May) and in grain filling (June). Heat above about 30 C during grain filling shortens filling and lowers grain weight. Frost kill risk is highest when winter has little snow cover and temperature drops below about -15 C at the crown. Good predecessors: pea, black or clean fallow, rapeseed, early-harvested legumes. Poor predecessors: wheat itself (take-all and root rots), late-harvested sunflower and sugar beet when soil is dried out. KMU 164 allows wheat to return to a field after at least 2 years.

# Maize
Sown late April to early May when soil is above 8-10 C, harvested September-October. Most sensitive around flowering and silking (July): a dry spell or days above 32-35 C then cut kernel set hard. Uses a lot of water in July-August, so it is risky on light soils in the south without irrigation. Leaves high residue and takes moisture, so a following winter wheat is sown into dry soil in dry autumns. Maize may be grown 2-3 years in a row per KMU 164, but monoculture builds up stalk rot, corn borer and weeds.

# Spring barley
Sown as early as soil allows (March-April), harvested July. Short cycle, so it often escapes the worst of the summer heat but suffers if spring is dry. Good cover or nurse crop. Gives an early harvest, which leaves time to prepare soil for winter crops. KMU 164: can follow itself only after a break, see the crop parameter table.

# Millet
Warm-season, drought tolerant, sown in May at soil above 12-14 C. Useful on light soils and as a late-sown crop after a failed one. Low yield level nationally (about 1-2 t/ha) but cheap to grow.

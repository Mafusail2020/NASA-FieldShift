# Sunflower
Sown April-May, harvested September. Deep roots reach water in autumn, so it tolerates drought better than maize, but it dries the soil profile deeply, which hurts the next crop. Return to the same field only after about 6-8 years to limit broomrape, Phomopsis, Sclerotinia and Verticillium. Wet cold weather at flowering and harvest raises Sclerotinia and Botrytis head rot risk. Poor predecessor for wheat in dry regions because of moisture depletion, and also for sugar beet and soybean (shared diseases).

# Soybean
Legume that fixes nitrogen, sown late April to May, harvested September. Sensitive to drought during flowering and pod fill (July-August). Needs seed inoculation on fields without soybean history. Good predecessor for winter wheat and maize (nitrogen credit, early harvest). Share of soybean in the rotation should stay limited (about 20-25 percent) to avoid Sclerotinia and nematode build-up.

# Pea
Cool-season legume, sown very early (March), harvested July. Heat above about 25-28 C during flowering and pod fill sharply lowers yield, so early sowing matters. Early harvest and nitrogen left in the soil make it one of the best predecessors for winter wheat. Return to the same field after 5-6 years to limit root rots and Fusarium.

# Winter rapeseed
Sown in August, needs enough soil water at sowing for even emergence; very sensitive to autumn drought. Winter survival depends on snow cover and fall growth. Good predecessor for wheat. Return after 4 years because of clubroot and Sclerotinia.
